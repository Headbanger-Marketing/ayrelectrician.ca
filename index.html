Before work begins

Write down every device before the work is scoped.

New appliances add to existing loads. A busy family home may hold more people and devices than it was first wired for, so kitchen, laundry and bedroom circuits can already carry more than planned before a new range or heat pump joins that load.

Before you ask for quotes, list who uses which room at what hours, which appliances run together in the evening, and which upgrades are only wished for. Ask each contractor to price the same combined list.

Which upgrades should be planned together in one project?

Group the work that shares a panel, a cable route or a wall opening, such as a charger, a heat pump and a new range. Ask the contractor which items gain from one visit and which can wait.

How do I compare quotes that cover different sets of work?

Ask every contractor to quote against one written list of equipment and locations. Where a quote leaves an item out or adds an allowance, ask for it to be named so the totals can be compared on equal terms.

Do the appliances I already own change what gets installed?

They can. Product sheets for a range, dryer or heat pump show how each connects, so keep the model details handy. A contractor can then assess whether the existing circuits suit them or new ones are needed.

Who can approve changes when several people live in the home?

The owner, or whoever holds authority over the property, usually approves the work, not each household member. Agree who that is, and who can open the door, before a contractor visits.

Once my enquiry is submitted, who decides what happens next?

First your enquiry is received and reviewed; the site decides nothing else. It is only reading matter, taking no part in visits or prices, nor in electrical work. If you then deal with a provider or Licensed Electrical Contractor, it confirms its name and licence with you ahead of any agreement, then scope and cost, and later timing.
