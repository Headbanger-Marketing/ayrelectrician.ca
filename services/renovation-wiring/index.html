Electrical decisions belong early in the renovation

A renovation changes how the space will be used and what equipment it must serve. Provide the drawings and appliance specifications before the electrical scope is finalized. A Licensed Electrical Contractor can assess retained wiring, new circuits and the proposed loads. Municipal building permissions and ESA electrical notification are separate matters, and the project may require both.

Prepare the scope for your Ayr property

Collect the drawings, the planned furniture and appliance list and any notes on heating, cooling or a small kitchen. Write down who will use the space and when, and photograph the area as it stands.

Ask the contractor which existing circuits would be extended and which new ones the converted space needs, and when walls must stay open for inspection. Ask how the work fits with the other trades.

What should the assessment establish?

What do the existing supply and circuits already serve before a garage or attic in Ayr becomes living space and adds new demand? The scope records that load, then places the planned lighting, receptacles and any heating or cooling equipment by location. Which parts of the earlier wiring stay, and on what basis? Those parts are listed with the contractor's reason for keeping each one. How will the new room share the household's existing load? That reasoning is written down plainly, beside the ESA notification and the stage at which walls are left open.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Will an attic bedroom need circuits separate from the rest of the home?

It rests on what the space will hold, so it is a question for the contractor's assessment rather than a rule. Describe the intended use and equipment, and ask for the circuit plan to be written down.

Does finishing a garage into a room need an ESA notification?

Almost all electrical work needs a notification, and a building permit is a separate matter. The contractor files the ESA notification, and you can ask both questions before the walls are closed.

Prepare your enquiry

Useful project details

  • Drawings and intended use of each space
  • Appliance and equipment model information
  • Construction schedule and other trades
  • Existing installation records if available
  • Person authorized to approve changes

Only photograph labels and locations that you can see safely. Do not remove electrical covers or approach damaged equipment to gather information.
